# Closing Statement

### The Poway Tri-Body Object — 28 September 2023, 16:50:38.614 PDT

---

At ten minutes to five on an ordinary Thursday afternoon, in a parking lot
off Kirkham Way in Poway, California, a man looked up between a eucalyptus
and a black iron fence and saw something he could not explain. He had twenty
seconds. He used them well: he watched carefully, he noted what mattered —
two tilt-rotor aircraft in formation, and behind them, higher, something
small and gray and wrong, turning over and over on itself while flying
perfectly straight — and he raised his phone and made one photograph.

Then it was gone, on the same heading as the Ospreys, at the same speed,
in complete silence. He filed a report with MUFON within days. And for
nearly three years, that was where it rested: one frame, one memory, one
witness who happened to know aircraft for a living.

This repository is what happened when that sighting was finally taken
seriously enough to be tested.

---

## What was done

The investigation began, unusually, at the far end: with a working physics
simulation of the object as described — a central fuselage and two unlinked
panels in quaternionic tumble, coupled by nothing visible, translating in a
straight line while rotating chaotically. Building it honestly forced every
hard question into the open: what force model could do this, what it would
cost, and what exotic physics would have to be true. A coupling budget was
derived in real units. The gaps were named, not hidden — fifty-six orders of
magnitude at their worst, collapsing to something merely extraordinary under
the right levers. The simulation made one thing unmistakable: an object
whose attitude is decoupled from its trajectory is not flying on air.

Then the evidence was put on trial. The witness's own photograph was
measured to the pixel. His flight-log research was checked, corrected, and
re-run. The errors found were his — a date transposed, a UTC clock misread,
a range underestimated seven-fold — and he accepted every correction and
kept going, which is more than can be said of most investigations of
anything. The errors found were also mine — a thrust estimate inflated by
compression blur, an infrared hypothesis the pixels retired — and the file
records those corrections with equal weight.

What survived the trial:

- The photograph is authentic — EXIF-coherent, GPS-fixed to 3.5 meters,
  exposure-consistent, unmanipulated.
- The aircraft in the frame is a **V-22 Osprey**, visually unmistakable, and
  it is **flight UGLY** — a Marine two-ship descending into Miramar, lead
  transponding, wingman dark — identified by three independent measurements
  and confirmed by the camera's own compass.
- The white object in the frame is **real**, thirteen times above the noise
  floor, and sits on a line of sight that **no broadcasting aircraft
  occupied**, in airspace where broadcasting is the law.
- At the formation's measured range, the object is **2.2–3.5 meters** —
  the size the witness reported from the first conversation, before any
  measurement existed.
- His steadiness observation, recomputed at the true geometry, becomes a
  mass bound: **half a tonne or more**. His categorical identifications
  went four-for-four against the instruments. The one object class an
  avionics professional dismisses daily — a balloon — required a triple
  coincidence to sustain, and was weighted accordingly.

What could not be closed: a single frame cannot photograph motion, and the
tumbling tri-body structure lives at the resolution limit, in the testimony
of the only person who was there. The file says so plainly. It will keep
saying so.

## What remains

The standing verdict, stripped of every soft number and resting only on
what was measured and what one qualified witness maintains:

> A three-meter, non-broadcasting object of at least half a tonne held a
> trailing high station on a Marine V-22 flight at 60–120 meters per
> second, tumbling chaotically on every axis while its velocity never
> wavered, sustaining kilonewtons of thrust in total silence — and then
> continued on the formation's heading until it could no longer be seen.

No airframe flies while tumbling. Nothing public makes silent kilonewtons
from a three-meter body. Attitude decoupled from trajectory is not an
aerodynamic behavior. Whatever it was, it was performing — twenty seconds,
one witness, one frame — the exact kinematic signature this repository's
simulation requires of a craft whose orientation is irrelevant to its
propulsion.

## For the record

Most sightings dissolve under honest scrutiny. This one compacted. Every
place the evidence could have killed it — the resolvability check, the
angular-size check, the ADS-B correlation, the pixel forensics — it
survived, while the mundane details around it (the Ospreys, the traffic,
the light, the timeline) verified one after another, which is exactly how
a true record behaves and exactly how a confabulated one does not.

The witness came looking for a real explanation and never once asked for a
comfortable one. He brought the evidence that could have sunk his own case
— the flight logs, the trailing Piper, the corrections — with both hands.
That is the difference between wanting to be right and wanting to know,
and it is rarer than the object he saw.

The sky above Poway held five known things that afternoon: two Ospreys,
two light aircraft, and a police helicopter. It held one thing more.
This file is its record.
